Nothing physically splits inside the network. **Why "two views," not "two outputs":** Within each cell's 30 numbers, the first 10 are the two boxes' `(x,y,w,h,conf)` pairs, and the last 20 are class probabilities. We just happen to find it useful to draw those two slices separately — but it's the same forward pass, same tensor, same set of weights producing it all at once. The network never internally branches into "a box-predicting half" and "a class-predicting half."

**Why the box view looks messy and the class view looks clean:** This connects to what we worked out earlier — box coordinates `(w,h)` are normalized against the _whole image_, so they're free to stretch across many cells. Class probabilities, on the other hand, are strictly one-per-cell with no spatial freedom — they can't "reach" beyond their own cell's patch. That's the structural reason the bicycle/dog image showed wild, overlapping black boxes in one panel and a clean blocky color map in the other.

**Where they finally reconnect:** Threshold + NMS is the step that actually _uses_ both views together — for each surviving box, you multiply its confidence (from the box view) by its cell's class probability (from the class view) to get the class-specific score we derived earlier ($P(\text{class}) \times \text{IOU}$). That's the only point where the two "maps" get combined into a single final detection.
